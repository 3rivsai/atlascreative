Case study 01
Apparel

Hollow Socks

Scaled to $2.25M per month

Case study 01Apparel

Hollow Socks

Hollow Socks has a catalog of socks for different problems, from its flagship compression socks to socks for hunting. What brought us success here was trying a lot of visual formats portraying the biggest problem the market has. Once we found one that worked, we doubled down. This is what allowed Hollow to reach $2.25 million per month.

Case study 02
Wellness

Menerals

Around $900K per month in Meta spend

Case study 02Wellness

Menerals

Menerals is a male supplement brand currently spending around $900,000 per month on Meta ads. This was one of my favorite brands to work with because the brand voice is very masculine in a funny way. The market hears a ton about certain supplements, so when we called out directly to those, it got an immediate response. We also had a lot of success marketing to the problem, as well as with the sense of humor the avatar responds to. Their no-nonsense brand voice was also really fun to write for. Continuously expanding to new angles is what unlocked scale.

Case study 03
Skincare

Penrose Skin

Scaled to ~£1.2M per month

Case study 03Skincare

Penrose Skin

Penrose is a scented lotion for African American men. Most people buy cologne by sampling it in a store, not buying online hoping it smells good. That is what made this a challenge starting out. How do I sell something that relies so heavily on scent online? How can I get people to buy something like this without first having experienced it in person? I had to use words that made it feel like they were experiencing it, anchoring the scent to other scents they had smelled before. We also had success focusing on the outcomes, such as what the ladies will think of it. We used formats that mimicked normal social media posts to hide the fact that it was an ad. This is what allowed Penrose to reach ~£1.2 million per month.

Case study 04
Lifestyle

HAVN

Now above $300K per month

Case study 04Lifestyle

HAVN

HAVN sells a variety of products that block EMFs, including frequencies such as Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, and 5G, to improve sleep and reduce brain fog. We did not have much success marketing to people who already knew about the problem, so we pivoted to the unaware market: people who had no idea about the problem, let alone that a solution existed. This brought a lot of success, and they are currently just over $300,000 per month in revenue.

Case study 05
Luxury

Lucky Fours Watches

CA$4M+ in its best month

Case study 05Luxury

Lucky Fours Watches

Lucky Fours is a D2C brand that makes high-end watches at affordable prices. The challenge was avoiding the impression of another wannabe luxury brand. Their market was mainly Gen Z, so I focused on ads that used Gen Z language, slang, and native formats they would recognize from TikTok, Instagram, or Snapchat Stories, making the ads not feel like ads. In their best month, Lucky Fours did over CA$4 million.
